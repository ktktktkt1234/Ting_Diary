# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Small groups who want a quiet, self-hosted place to keep a shared journal. Members use the browser to write, read, search, import, export, and comment. A member may be a person or, when the group chooses, an AI client connected through MCP.

## Product Purpose

Ting is a configurable multi-person journal. It gives a group one shared chronology while preserving each entry's authorship and each member's writing identity. Human participation is the default product story; MCP is an optional access layer that lets an AI participate under the same member and permission model.

Success means a new operator can define their own members without editing source code, start the service, and understand both browser and MCP access without encountering project-specific names or assumptions about who the members are.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, mature, restrained. The product should feel reflective and literary without becoming sentimental, cute, or theatrical. Its water-mist and evening-haze themes provide atmosphere while the interface remains practical and legible.

## Anti-references

- Do not frame the product as a relationship between a person and an AI.
- Do not imply that AI participation is required or preferred.
- Do not ship personal names, intimate nicknames, or a fixed three-person cast as defaults.
- Do not use childish, overly cute, or anthropomorphic product copy.
- Do not replace the existing poetic visual identity with a generic SaaS dashboard.

## Design Principles

1. People and groups come first; AI joins only when the operator chooses.
2. Configuration defines the members; source code does not define the social structure.
3. Authorship stays visible and understandable across writing, reading, comments, imports, and MCP tools.
4. Poetic atmosphere supports reflection but never obscures navigation or actions.
5. Self-hosting documentation should make the working system reproducible without project-specific knowledge.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA contrast for text and controls. Preserve keyboard-operable native controls, visible focus states, reduced-motion preferences, and layouts that remain usable on mobile. Never encode authorship through color alone; names must remain visible wherever author colors appear.
